Core problem: Hospitals are penalized financially (e.g., under CMS's Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program)
when diabetic patients are readmitted within 30 days of discharge. 
The hospital quality and operations teams need to understand which patients, conditions, and discharge practices most strongly predict early readmission, 
so clinical and discharge-planning interventions can be targeted where they will have the most impact.

Guiding analytical questions:

1Q.What is the overall 30-day readmission rate, and how does it compare with the >30-day and no-readmission groups?
2Q.Which age bands have the highest 30-day readmission rates?
3Q.Does length of stay (time_in_hospital) correlate with a higher chance of early readmission?
4Q.How do the number of prior inpatient, outpatient, and emergency visits relate to readmission risk (a proxy for chronic high-utilizers)?
5Q.Which admission type (emergency, urgent, elective) and admission source carry the highest readmission rates?
6Q.Does the discharge disposition (e.g., discharged to home vs. transferred to another facility) affect readmission likelihood?
7Q.How does the number of medications prescribed during the stay relate to readmission — does polypharmacy increase risk?
8Q.Is there a relationship between A1C test results (and whether the result changed treatment) and readmission rate?
9Q.Do patients with more diagnosed conditions (number_diagnoses) have a measurably higher readmission rate?
10Q.Which medical specialty (e.g., cardiology, internal medicine, family practice) of the admitting physician shows the highest readmission rates among their patients?
11Q.Does a change in diabetes medication during the encounter (change = "Ch") correlate with reduced or increased readmission?
12Q.Are there race or gender disparities in readmission rates that warrant equity-focused review?
13Q.What is the combined risk profile (e.g., elderly + high prior-utilization + long stay) that identifies the highest-risk patients for targeted discharge planning?
14Q.What actionable discharge-process and care-coordination changes could reduce the 30-day readmission rate?
